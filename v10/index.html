"Foundations of the Barely Readable" by Prof. Georg Can'tor

📅 Tuesday, March 28, 2006 | 11:47 PM Category: Textbook Reviews
★★☆☆☆ Geneva Convention Violation

You guys. YOU GUYS. I thought I had seen it all. I thought after seven years in this program, no textbook could hurt me anymore. I was wrong.

Prof. Georg Can'tor has written a 900-page tome on set theory and real analysis that somehow manages to define "set" on page 412. What are the first 411 pages about, you ask? Vibes. Pure mathematical vibes. There are paragraphs that read like fortune cookies written by someone who just failed their qualifying exams.

Chapter 3 is titled "The Nature of Infinity" and contains exactly one theorem, fourteen philosophical tangents, and a two-page footnote about the author's trip to Tuscany. The proof of the one theorem is "left to the reader," naturally. The theorem is the Cantor diagonal argument. HE LEFT HIS OWN THEOREM AS AN EXERCISE.

The index lists "despair" but not "convergence."

"Measure Theory for the Gifted and Unbothered" by Dr. Henri Lèbes-GUESS

📅 Saturday, March 25, 2006 | 3:22 AM Category: Textbook Reviews
★☆☆☆☆ This Book Owes Me Therapy

[Guest review by my friend Jasper. I let him have the keyboard and I am not responsible for what follows.]

Okay FIRST OF ALL, I did not get a blowout, a manicure, and an iced oat latte to sit in the library at 3 AM reading a chapter called "Sigma-Algebras and the Spaces Between Us." THE SPACES BETWEEN US?? Sir, this is a MATH TEXTBOOK, not a Nicholas Sparks novel, and honestly Nicholas Sparks would NEVER do this to me.

Dr. Henri Lèbes-GUESS (and yes I'm calling him that because every proof in this book is just him GUESSING and then writing "it can be shown" like bestie NO IT CANNOT, I have been TRYING to show it for FOUR HOURS) decided that measurable functions don't need motivation. Just definitions. Dropped on you like a piano in a cartoon. Splat. There you go. Figure it out.

Page 206 literally says "the intuition here is clear." THE INTUITION IS NOT CLEAR, HENRI. NOTHING HAS BEEN CLEAR SINCE CHAPTER ONE. I am sitting in this fluorescent-lit dungeon with mascara running down my face trying to understand why the Vitali set broke my understanding of length and you're telling me the INTUITION IS CLEAR?

I gave this book one star and honestly that's generous. The one star is for the font. It's a nice font. Everything else? Absolutely incarcerable.

"A Gentle Introduction (It Isn't)" by Augustin-Louis Cau-She-Didn't

📅 Wednesday, March 20, 2006 | 7:15 PM Category: Textbook Reviews
★★★☆☆ War Crime

The title of this book is a lie so audacious it should be studied by political scientists. "A Gentle Introduction to Real Analysis." Gentle? GENTLE? Chapter 1, page 1, sentence 1: "Let X be a complete separable metrizable locally compact Hausdorff space." That is the INTRODUCTION. That is the author saying HELLO.

Dr. Cau-She-Didn't has a unique pedagogical philosophy: she believes that if you simply present all the theorems in the most abstract form possible with zero examples, the student's brain will "naturally reconstruct the intuition." This is written in the preface. She actually typed that with her fingers and thought "yes, good, publish."

The exercises range from "trivial consequence of the definition" to "open research problem" with absolutely no indication of which is which. Exercise 3.7 took me six hours. Exercise 3.8 is apparently unsolved. I emailed the author and she said "good luck!" with a smiley face.

There are no pictures in this book. Not one. Not even a number line. I think she's philosophically opposed to visual aids.

"Topological Diversions and Algebraic Regrets" by Karl Why-erstrass & Sofya Kova-LEGS-kaya

📅 Friday, March 15, 2006 | 9:03 AM Category: Textbook Reviews
★★☆☆☆ Geneva Convention Violation

A co-authored textbook should, in theory, combine the strengths of both authors. In practice, this book combines Why-erstrass's obsession with pathological counterexamples and Kova-LEGS-kaya's deep belief that differential equations belong in every chapter, even the ones about point-set topology.

The book can't decide what it wants to be. Chapter 4 is about uniform convergence. Chapter 5 is about fluid dynamics. Chapter 6 returns to sequences but acts like Chapter 5 never happened. It's like the two authors wrote separate books and someone shuffled the pages together.

The worst part is the "Historical Notes" sections, which appear every thirty pages and are clearly just Why-erstrass settling personal grudges with other mathematicians. There's a full-page footnote in Chapter 8 that's essentially a diss track aimed at Kronecker. It's petty, unprofessional, and honestly the most entertaining part of the book.

The errata sheet is eleven pages long. The book is three hundred pages. Do the math. (If you can, after reading this book, you've done better than most.)
