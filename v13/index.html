The Worst Analysis Textbooks Ever Published

Documenting the textbooks that made grad school a waking nightmare. Rated on a 5-star scale where 5 stars means "only mildly traumatizing."

“Principles of Abstraction Beyond Necessity” by Prof. Bernhard Ree-WRONG
Posted: Wednesday, April 5, 2006 at 10:38 PM  |  Category: Textbook Roasts
★★☆☆☆   2/5 — "Academically Hostile"

I want to meet the editorial board that greenlit this book. Not to yell at them. Just to look into their eyes and try to understand how a 700-page analysis text can go 200 pages without a single concrete example.

Prof. Ree-WRONG opens Chapter 1 with "We assume the reader is fluent in category theory, homological algebra, and advanced set-theoretic forcing." This is supposed to be an introductory course text. The prerequisite list has prerequisites. It's prerequisites all the way down.

The crowning moment is Theorem 4.19, which he calls "elementary." The proof spans fourteen pages, references three papers that haven't been published yet (one of them is his own, still under review), and ends with "the remaining details are straightforward." I spent a week on those "straightforward" details. They were not straightforward. I aged visibly.

The index contains the entry "intuition — see: unnecessary." I thought it was a typo. It is not a typo. He did that on purpose.

“Integration: A Spiritual Journey” by Dr. Pierre-Simon LaGRAVE Mistake
Posted: Saturday, April 1, 2006 at 2:14 AM  |  Category: Textbook Roasts
★☆☆☆☆   1/5 — "This Book Violated My Rights"

[Guest post by Jasper. He has feelings. All of the feelings.]

OKAY. So there I was, skinny jeans pressed, chai in hand, highlighters arranged chromatically — fully prepared to be a serious academic. I open this book. Chapter 1 is called "Surrendering to the Integral." SURRENDERING. Like this is a yoga retreat and not a graduate mathematics course. I did NOT apply to this program to find my inner Lebesgue, Dr. LaGRAVE.

By Chapter 3 he's writing things like "one must feel the convergence before one can prove it" and I am literally on the floor of the math lounge having a crisis because I can't feel ANYTHING except the existential dread of my funding running out. Every proof is wrapped in flowery metaphors. He compares the dominated convergence theorem to "a river finding its destined ocean." DESTINED OCEAN?? Honey, the only thing I'm converging toward is a breakdown.

And the EXERCISES. Exercise 7.3 says "Meditate on the properties of L-p spaces and write a reflective paragraph." A REFLECTIVE PARAGRAPH. In a MATH class. I didn't moisturize for this. I didn't spend forty-five minutes on my hair for this. I came here to compute integrals and instead I'm being asked to journal about Fubini's theorem like it's my therapist.

One star. The one star is because he at least had the decency to use nice paper stock. The pages have a lovely weight to them. The content printed on those pages? Incarcerable.

“Compactness and Other Lies I Tell My Students” by Prof. Emmy No-ther Way
Posted: Tuesday, March 28, 2006 at 6:55 PM  |  Category: Textbook Roasts
★★★☆☆   3/5 — "Educational Malpractice"

I will give Prof. No-ther Way exactly one thing: the title is honest. This is indeed a book of lies. She opens the preface with "this textbook aims to build the student's geometric intuition for topology" and then proceeds to include zero pictures for 450 pages. Not a diagram. Not a sketch. Not even a doodle in the margins. Just axioms, definitions, and an aura of menace.

Her proof style is what I call "the vanishing act." Step 1 will be clearly stated. Step 2 will reference Step 1. Step 3 will say "by a similar argument to Step 1 but applied to the dual situation, accounting for the necessary modifications." WHAT MODIFICATIONS? You can't just invoke "necessary modifications" like a spell. This isn't Hogwarts. Show your work, Professor.

Chapter 9 introduces seventeen equivalent definitions of compactness across four pages and then says "the reader should verify these are equivalent." That's not a textbook exercise, that's a dissertation topic. My advisor laughed when I showed him. Then he stopped laughing because he realized he assigned this book.

The three stars are because, if you already understand the material, the book is actually quite elegant. Which defeats the entire purpose of a textbook. It's like a cookbook that only works if you already know how to cook.

“Sequences, Series, and Suffering: A Complete Course” by Drs. Gro-THIN-dieck & Errdös
Posted: Friday, March 22, 2006 at 11:20 AM  |  Category: Textbook Roasts
★★☆☆☆   2/5 — "Academically Hostile"

A co-authored disaster. Gro-THIN-dieck handled the theory chapters and Errdös handled the problem sets, and it's immediately obvious they never spoke to each other during the writing process. The theory chapters are written in a style so abstract that actual numbers appear approximately never. Then the problem sets cheerfully ask you to compute specific sums using techniques from the chapter, which contained no techniques. Only philosophy.

Gro-THIN-dieck's chapters keep escalating in generality for no apparent reason. Chapter 6 starts with sequences of real numbers and by page 4 you're working in a general topological vector space over an arbitrary valued field. Sir, we were counting. We were literally just counting things in order. How did we get here.

Meanwhile, Errdös clearly wrote his problem sets at 3 AM powered by nothing but espresso and spite. Problem 8.14 is flagged as "routine" and requires four lemmas that aren't in the book. The solutions manual (sold separately, naturally) contains the phrase "this is left as a straightforward exercise" FOR A PROBLEM IN THE SOLUTIONS MANUAL. A solution that is itself an exercise. It's fractal homework. Homework all the way down.
