MathKiller_99 Cambridge, MA ★ 12 reviews • 48 helpful votes
Reviewed March 14, 2026

I bought this book thinking "real analysis" meant it would be practical and grounded in reality. I was wrong. Nothing about this book is grounded in anything resembling the real world. The author appears to have written it while astral-projecting to a dimension where human empathy does not exist.

Chapter 1 starts with "We begin with a review of basic set theory" and proceeds to review absolutely nothing. It is not a review. It is an ambush. By page 12, I had encountered the phrase "the proof is left as an exercise" three times. Three times! We hadn't even gotten to analysis yet. We were still in the TABLE OF CONTENTS REGION of mathematical knowledge and already I was expected to prove things on my own.

The word "clearly" appears on average once every 2.3 pages. I counted. Nothing that follows the word "clearly" has ever been clear. Not once. "Clearly, the sequence converges" — CLEARLY? TO WHOM? Not to me, Walter. Not to me.

I would give zero stars if I could. The binding is nice though. One star for the binding.

SassyProof_xo West Hollywood, CA ★ 31 reviews • 206 helpful votes • Elite '26 ✨
Reviewed February 22, 2026

First of all, the NERVE of this textbook showing up to my semester looking like that. 800 pages? In THIS economy? I didn't sign up for this — literally, my advisor enrolled me without asking. I walked into lecture, saw this BRICK on every desk, and immediately texted my group chat "girls I am NOT going to make it."

Let me set the scene. It's week three. I've done my skincare routine, I've got my oat milk latte, I've got my color-coded notes spread out like a Pinterest board. I'm READY. I open to Chapter 2: "Metric Spaces and Their Properties." Gorgeous title. Love the mystery. And then — AND THEN — the very first sentence says, "We assume the reader is familiar with the basics of point-set topology." We ASSUME? Baby, you cannot assume ANYTHING about me. I am full of surprises and none of them involve point-set topology.

The diagrams. Oh, the DIAGRAMS. There are exactly four diagrams in 800 pages. Four! I have more diagrams on my vision board. One of them is just two circles overlapping and it's labeled Figure 2.1 as if we should be GRATEFUL. I spent $211 on this. I could have gotten like... forty-two oat milk lattes. I could have gone to brunch SEVEN TIMES. Instead I got a book where the index entry for "example" says "see: nowhere, they don't exist."

By Chapter 5 I was openly weeping in the math library. A grad student asked if I was okay and I said "define okay. Define it RIGOROUSLY, because apparently that's what we DO now." She walked away. I don't blame her. This book has made me insufferable.

The author dedicates the book to "my students, who inspired me to write this." Sir, your students inspired you the way a car crash inspires traffic laws. They did not want this. Nobody wanted this. I am going to leave this book in a Little Free Library and simply ruin a stranger's afternoon.

One star because zero is "not an element of the natural numbers," as this book reminded me seventeen times. I hate it here.

AlmostTenured Chicago, IL ★ 7 reviews • 89 helpful votes
Reviewed January 9, 2026

I am a fourth-year PhD student. I have passed my qualifying exams. I have taught undergraduates. I have published a paper. And this book made me feel like I have never seen a number before in my life.

The preface says this text is "suitable for advanced undergraduates." I want to meet these undergraduates. I want to study them. They are not human. No human undergraduate can read the sentence "the Lebesgue outer measure of a Vitali set is necessarily non-measurable, as the reader can trivially verify" and trivially verify ANYTHING. I couldn't trivially verify my own name after reading Chapter 3.

There is a theorem on page 247 whose proof begins with "This follows immediately from Theorem 4.12, Corollary 6.3, Lemma 8.7, and the preceding discussion." THE PRECEDING DISCUSSION IS FORTY PAGES LONG. That is not a proof, that is a scavenger hunt. That is an escape room designed by someone who does not want you to escape.

My favorite part is the exercises. Exercise 1 is always "verify the claims of this section." Which claims? ALL OF THEM. There are NINETEEN claims in Section 5.2 alone. Exercise 2 is always something from an entirely different branch of mathematics that the authors assume you know because they assume you are a being of pure mathematical energy and not a person who sometimes forgets to eat lunch.

The errata document is forty-three pages long. The book is three hundred and twelve pages long. The ratio of errors to pages is a number this book itself would call "non-trivial."

EpsilonNightmare Ann Arbor, MI ★ 4 reviews • 27 helpful votes
Reviewed March 2, 2026

Before I opened this book, I was a happy person. I had hobbies. I went outside. I knew the warmth of the sun on my face. That was five months ago. Now I live in the library basement and I can't stop muttering "dual space" under my breath. My roommate asked me to move out. I understand. I would also ask me to move out.

The book opens with "Let H be a Hilbert space." Just like that. No hello, no introduction, no gentle easing-in. "Let H be a Hilbert space" as if I just HAVE one lying around. As if I keep a Hilbert space in my back pocket next to my wallet and my will to live, both of which this book has drained entirely.

Chapter 7 is titled "Spectral Theory." I thought it would be about spectra, like colors, like something beautiful and visual. It was not. It was about operators. It was about BOUNDED LINEAR OPERATORS ON INFINITE-DIMENSIONAL SPACES. There were no colors. There was no beauty. There was only the Hahn-Banach theorem and its seventeen consequences, each more incomprehensible than the last.

On page 341, there is a typo where the authors wrote "the reader will enjoy verifying..." I stared at that sentence for eleven minutes. Enjoy. ENJOY. I have not enjoyed anything since September. I tried to watch a movie last week and paused it to check whether the plot was converging uniformly. It was not. Neither was I.

If you are considering buying this book, instead consider: walking into the sea, learning to weave, adopting a cat. All better uses of your time and money. One star. The single star represents the dying light of my academic career.
