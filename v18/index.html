PL
ProofLurker Ithaca, NY ★ 18 reviews • 271 helpful votes
Reviewed March 28, 2026

Augustin-Louis Sloshy has written the textbook equivalent of being slowly lowered into cold water while someone reads your failures aloud. The preface promises "an accessible introduction to the foundations of analysis." Accessible to WHOM, Augustin? To omniscient beings? To superintelligent AI? Because it is not accessible to me, a human with a bachelor's degree and a dwindling sense of self-worth.

The first chapter is titled "Preliminaries." It is 90 pages long. The preliminaries are longer than most textbooks' entire contents. By page 40 I realized the "preliminaries" section contained three theorems that took Cauchy himself years to prove, presented here as things I should already know. I did not already know them. I do not know anything anymore.

My personal low point was Exercise 2.17, which reads: "The reader should find the following straightforward." I did not find it straightforward. I found it on the floor of my apartment at 3 AM, surrounded by crumpled paper and the remains of my self-esteem. One star. The star is for the font choice, which is admittedly pleasant.

DV
DivaDelta_xo Silver Lake, CA ★ 44 reviews • 1,320 helpful votes • Elite '26 ✨
Reviewed March 15, 2026

Okay so let me paint the picture. It is a Tuesday. I am wearing my best studying cardigan — the camel one, very academic, very "I take this seriously." I have my matcha. I have my noise-canceling headphones playing lo-fi beats. I am PREPARED. I am going to CONQUER harmonic analysis today. I open Chapter 3: "Singular Integrals." Gorgeous. Mysterious. I'm intrigued. And then Fourier-Real and Steinbroke hit me with a sentence that begins — I am not making this up — "It is a well-known fact that..."

A WELL-KNOWN FACT? To WHOM, Joseph? To the ILLUMINATI? Because it is not well-known to me, a person who has been studying mathematics for SIX YEARS and still cannot look at a Calderón-Zygmund kernel without hyperventilating. I texted my study group "I am being gaslit by a textbook" and three of them replied "same." Three! We are all being collectively deceived by this book and its audacious claims about what is "well-known."

The notation. THE NOTATION. There are seventeen different hats in this book and none of them are fashion accessories. Every function gets a hat. Some functions get TWO hats. There is a function on page 412 wearing what I can only describe as a mathematical fascinator. I spent forty-five minutes trying to figure out what f-hat-tilde-bar means and the answer was apparently "obvious from context." NOTHING about a function in a triple disguise is obvious from context, Elias!

Chapter 7 is called "Littlewood-Paley Theory." Cute name. Sounds like a cottage in the Cotswolds. It is NOT a cottage. It is a DUNGEON. I went in thinking about little woods and came out thinking about dropping out to become a florist. Do florists need to understand dyadic decompositions? They do not. They understand FLOWERS, which are beautiful and do not require the Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem to appreciate.

The book is dedicated to "the next generation of analysts." Sir, there will BE no next generation if you keep writing books like this. We are an endangered species. I am giving this one star because the cover is a nice shade of purple and I am nothing if not fair. One star for aesthetics, zero stars for the war crime that is Chapter 9.

NB
NullAndBored New Haven, CT ★ 9 reviews • 156 helpful votes
Reviewed February 14, 2026

René Baire-ly Trying has written a book that lives up to his name in the worst possible way. The Lebesgue integral is presented with the emotional warmth of a tax audit. Every proof begins with "Consider the following" and what follows is never something anyone would willingly consider. It's always a sigma-algebra or a Borel set or some other construction that sounds like an alien species from a sci-fi novel I would not read.

The book contains zero diagrams. ZERO. In 450 pages of measure theory, not a single picture. I asked my professor if this was a printing error. She laughed for thirty seconds and then looked very sad. Apparently it is intentional. The author believes diagrams "impede rigorous thinking." You know what impedes MY rigorous thinking? Not having a single visual cue in a book about MEASURING THINGS. We invented pictures for a reason, René.

The half star is because Chapter 11 on the Radon-Nikodym theorem is, against all odds, almost comprehensible. I suspect it was written by a different person. Perhaps a hostage who was briefly allowed to write one coherent chapter before being returned to the notation dungeon.

KW
KernelOfTruth Göttingen, Germany ★ 22 reviews • 410 helpful votes
Reviewed January 30, 2026

I have been a postdoc for four years. I have refereed papers for Inventiones. I have attended conferences where people argue about the axiom of choice over canapés. I considered myself a competent mathematician. And then I opened this book to page one, read the sentence "We work in a separable infinite-dimensional complex Hilbert space throughout, unless otherwise noted," and felt my competence evaporate like a puddle in the Sahara.

Bana-CRACK and Reisz-urrection subscribe to what I can only call the "blitzkrieg school" of mathematical exposition. There is no buildup, no motivation, no gentle ramp. You are simply dropped into the deep end of an infinite-dimensional pool. The table of contents reads like a threat list: "Compact Operators," "Spectral Measures," "Unbounded Operators," "Semigroups." Each chapter hits you harder than the last. There is no recovery period between chapters. There is no mercy.

The font is 9-point. NINE POINT. I am a 31-year-old man and I needed a magnifying glass. The subscripts have subscripts. There are superscripts so small they are essentially theoretical objects — you must take their existence on faith, which is ironic for a book that claims to be about rigorous mathematics.

My girlfriend left me during Chapter 8 because I "wasn't present anymore." She was right. I was in a Banach space. I have been in a Banach space for five months. It is cold here and the dual space extends infinitely in every direction. Send help. One star.

TF
TestFunctionFanatic Paris, France ★ 6 reviews • 88 helpful votes
Reviewed March 3, 2026

Laurent Schwarz-enegger promised distributions and delivered destruction. The book has no index. Allow me to repeat that for emphasis: a 550-page mathematics textbook with NO INDEX. When I need to find the definition of a tempered distribution, I cannot look it up. I must simply wander through the pages like a medieval pilgrim seeking enlightenment, except the enlightenment never comes and the pages all look the same because there are no section headings after Chapter 4.

The author introduces wavelets on page 300 with the sentence "We now turn to a topic the reader has surely been anticipating." I was not anticipating wavelets. I was anticipating the end of my suffering, which this book seems constitutionally opposed to providing. The wavelet section assumes familiarity with "the standard Littlewood-Paley decomposition," which was not covered in this book, in any prerequisite, or apparently in any text written for mortal humans.

The errata PDF is 67 pages. The book is 550 pages. That is an error rate that would shut down a pharmaceutical company. And yet it remains a "recommended text" in my department, which tells you everything you need to know about the state of mathematical education. One star, and I'm being charitable because at least the binding hasn't fallen apart yet. Give it time.
