The Worst Analysis Textbooks

An Annotated Bibliography of Texts Whose Pedagogical Value Is Bounded Above by Zero1

Compiled by the Standing Committee on Curricular Malpractice

Submitted: Reluctantly · Revised: Under Threat · Published: Despite Everything

§

Abstract

We present a selective, non-exhaustive survey of analysis textbooks whose collective impact on the field may be characterized less as "advancing human knowledge" and more as "generating billable therapy hours." Each entry was evaluated by a panel of recovering analysis students using a standardized rubric2 measuring clarity (absent), accessibility (theoretical), and the probability of inducing an audible groan per page (high). Star ratings range from 0.5 to 2.0, where 2.0 denotes "bad but survivable" and 0.5 denotes "a document that should be studied by the Hague."

Reviewed Texts

I

The Lebesgue Integral: A Journey Without Maps

By Emile Bore-All & Fatool Luzin-Around

Springer-Verlag Rejects, 2nd ed., 2018. 612 pp.

1.0 / 5.0

“The authors open Chapter 1 with the sentence: ‘We assume the reader is familiar with the basics.’ The basics, it turns out, include all of graduate-level measure theory, several results from ergodic theory, and an emotional resilience that I frankly do not possess. By page 40, I had underlined every sentence. This did not help. Nothing helps.”

— Dr. K. Petrossian, Book Review Editor, Bulletin of Symbolic Suffering
II

Real Analysis in Theory and in Pain

By G. Folland-Over-and-Die

Cambridge Distress Press, 4th ed., 2021. 488 pp.

0.5 / 5.0

“Let me set the scene. It is 1 a.m. I am in the math library. I have been here since noon. My iced coffee is warm. My laptop is at 4%. And this book—THIS book—has just told me, on page 311, to ‘refer to the remark following Proposition 2.14.’ There IS no remark following Proposition 2.14. There is a blank quarter-page and then Proposition 2.15 starts. I flipped back and forth seventeen times. I held the page up to the light like it was the Shroud of Turin. Nothing. The remark does not exist. It has never existed. I am being gaslit by a TEXTBOOK. I literally screamed in the library. A girl at the next table said ‘mood’ and went back to her own suffering. Absolutely not. I am switching to data science. Goodbye.”

— Marcus V., via an unhinged 2 a.m. Goodreads review3
III

An Invitation to Harmonic Analysis (The Invitation Was a Trap)

By Elias Stein-of-My-Existence & Rami Shaka-Can’t

Princeton Univ. Stress, 1st ed., 2019. 530 pp.

1.5 / 5.0

“The word ‘straightforward’ appears forty-one times. I counted. Not once was the thing that followed it straightforward. Theorem 8.3 is described as a ‘simple corollary’ of a result that requires three lemmas, a change of variables, and what I can only describe as faith. The exercises range from ‘trivial if you already know the answer’ to ‘open problem disguised with a homework number.’”

— Assoc. Prof. W. Lindqvist, in a tenure review that was supposed to be about something else
IV

Partial Differential Equations and Partial Understanding

By Lawrence See-Evans & Fritz Jawn

Academic Duress, 3rd ed., 2020. 740 pp.

1.0 / 5.0

“This book is 740 pages long. The table of contents alone is 18 pages. The index is missing the letter ‘S,’ which is unfortunate given that the book is about, among other things, Sobolev spaces. The proof of existence and uniqueness in Chapter 6 occupies 28 pages, at the end of which the authors note that ‘a shorter proof exists but is less illuminating.’ I would like to be illuminated by the shorter proof. I would like to be illuminated by anything at this point.”

— Prof. N. Al-Rashid, internal departmental memo marked "DO NOT CIRCULATE"4
V

Sequences, Series, and Shattered Dreams: A Rigorous Approach

By Nikolai Loba-Chevy-Sky, Sofya Kova-Nope-skaya, & P. L. Cheby-Shove

Dover Disappointments, 7th ed., 2015. 394 pp.

2.0 / 5.0

“At seven editions, one might expect the errors to have been corrected. One would be wrong. The errata for the 7th edition references corrections from the 3rd edition that were never implemented. Theorem 5.9 contradicts Theorem 5.12. Both are cited in the proof of Theorem 5.15. The acknowledgments thank ‘the many students whose feedback shaped this edition,’ which I interpret as a confession.”

— Dr. J. Håkansson, reviewing for Mathematical Intelligencer (rejected for "excessive hostility")

A Note from the Committee

The Standing Committee on Curricular Malpractice does not accept unsolicited submissions. This has not stopped anyone. Our inbox currently contains 2,814 nominations, 43 cease-and-desist letters, and one marriage proposal from a reader who said our review of Sequences, Series, and Shattered Dreams "finally made them feel seen."5

Future editions of this catalog are planned, though the Committee notes that the supply of terrible analysis textbooks shows no signs of convergence—pointwise or otherwise.
