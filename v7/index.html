No. 01
Principles of Painful Convergence
Henri Lame-besgue
Review No. 001

Principles of Painful Convergence

Henri Lame-besgue

★★★★★

One might charitably describe Lame-besgue’s opus as “ambitious.” One might also describe a house fire as “warm.” The author introduces seventeen distinct notations for the integral in the first forty pages, then quietly abandons fourteen of them by Chapter 4 without explanation, as if hoping the reader simply won’t notice the carnage.

The proofs are “left as exercises” with such alarming frequency that one begins to suspect the author himself could not complete them. Theorem 6.11 references a lemma from a paper that, as far as any living mathematician can determine, does not exist.

— Prof. R. Caldwell, Dept. of Mathematics, fictitious university

“The index lists ‘hope’ on page 1. There is no page 1 entry for hope.”

— A despairing reviewer

No. 02
Measure Theory & the Abyss
Georg Can’t-or
Review No. 002

Measure Theory & the Abyss

Georg Can’t-or

★★★★★

Okay bestie let me be SO clear about this book. I sat down with my oat milk latte, my highlighters color-coordinated by theorem type, my little sticky tabs—I was READY. I was going to be a measure theory girlie. I was going to UNDERSTAND sigma-algebras and look cute doing it.

Page twelve. PAGE TWELVE. This man introduces the Vitali set like it’s nothing, like he’s not about to unravel my entire sense of mathematical reality. Not me having an existential crisis in the campus Starbucks because apparently you can’t measure every set??? The barista asked if I was okay and I said “define okay because according to Chapter 2, even the definition of a definition is under dispute.”

“This book didn’t just lose me—it reported me missing.”

The way he writes “it is easily seen that” before the single hardest paragraph I have ever encountered in print. Sir, nothing about Carathéodory’s extension theorem is “easily seen.” I have SEEN things after reading this chapter. I see them when I close my eyes. I am not the same person I was before Definition 3.4.

— Tristan K., first-year grad student & emotional casualty

“Figure 1 is mislabeled. There is no Figure 2. Figure 3 references Figure 2.”

— Errata sheet, page 1 of 47

No. 03
Functional Dysfunction: A Hilbert Space Tragedy
Ninth “Corrected” Edition
Bernhard Rie-man-no
Review No. 003

Functional Dysfunction:
A Hilbert Space Tragedy

Bernhard Rie-man-no

★★★★★

Now in its ninth “corrected” edition, Rie-man-no’s treatise on functional analysis has achieved something genuinely remarkable: each new edition introduces more errors than it resolves. The errata for the eighth edition is longer than the original Chapter 5. The errata for the errata was published as a separate pamphlet.

The author’s approach to Hilbert spaces can be summarized as follows: define everything twice, prove nothing once. The section on compact operators opens with “We recall from undergraduate analysis…” and then proceeds to recall material that was never taught at any university on this planet. I checked. I emailed seven departments.

Theorem 8.3 states a result and then proves a completely different result. No one has noticed because no one has ever reached Theorem 8.3.

— Dr. M. Ashworth, who regrets her career choices

“Proof: Obvious. (It was not obvious.)”

— Every analysis textbook ever written

No. 04
Review No. 004

Topology of Despair: Open Sets, Closed Minds

Felix Housedog (née Hausdorff)

★★★★★

Housedog has produced what can only be described as a topological crime scene. The text opens with a 200-word sentence defining “neighborhood” that manages to be both circular and incomplete. His neighborhoods are places you would not want to live.

By Chapter 6, the author is freely mixing metaphor with mathematics: “Consider the set U, which yearns to be open.” Sir, sets do not yearn. I, however, yearn for a refund.

— Prof. J. Nakamura, review submitted under duress

Topology of Despair: Open Sets, Closed Minds
Felix Housedog
“The Hausdorff property: the ability to separate any two points. This book cannot separate any two ideas.”

A colleague once described this text as “the literary equivalent of a non-Hausdorff space—you cannot distinguish between any of its points.” I have nothing to add to this assessment except my resignation letter.

— Anonymous, tenure-track faculty

No. 05
Complex Variables, Simple Failures
A treatise in confusion
Pierre-Simon Lack-place
Review No. 005

Complex Variables, Simple Failures

Pierre-Simon Lack-place

★★★★★

Lack-place has authored what purports to be an introduction to complex analysis but functions more accurately as a psychological evaluation tool. If you can read past Chapter 7 without incident, you may lack the capacity for suffering that defines the human condition.

The treatment of contour integration is presented with all the clarity of a fog machine in a library. Residues are introduced on page 340 and then “used freely” on page 38—yes, three hundred pages earlier—in what I can only assume is a printing error that no one at the publishing house cared enough to fix.

“The only thing analytic about this book is the therapy I needed after reading it.”

Special mention must be made of Figure 12, which depicts what the caption calls “a simply connected domain” but which more closely resembles a drawing of a horse done from memory by someone who has never seen a horse. The Cauchy integral formula deserved better. We all deserved better.

— Dr. S. Petrov, three-time survivor of this textbook

“This book made me switch to the humanities. I am now happy.”

— Former mathematics major, current pottery instructor
