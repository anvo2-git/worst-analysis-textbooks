Principles of Functional Dysfunction

by Prof. Stefan Banacheck

★ 1.4 289 reviews
R
Dr. Rachel Okonkwo Chicago, IL 52 reviews · 141 helpful votes
★ ★ ☆ ☆ ☆

I have been a professor for twenty-two years and I assigned this textbook exactly once. Within three weeks I received a formal complaint co-signed by the entire class, a petition with 84 signatures from students in adjacent departments who had merely heard about the book, and a hand-delivered letter from the campus ombudsman asking me to "reconsider my pedagogical choices." The exercises include "Prove or disprove" problems where neither option leads anywhere productive. Banacheck's definition of a bounded operator spans four pages and references three other definitions that appear later in the text. A temporal impossibility. Two stars because the binding is nice.

★ 1.0 518 reviews

★ ☆ ☆ ☆ ☆

The title is not metaphorical. There is genuinely no mercy anywhere in this book. Chapter 1 is titled "Prerequisites" and it is 200 pages long. The author uses seven different notations for the same contour integral, sometimes on the same page, and addresses this in a footnote that simply reads "the reader will not be confused." I was confused. I am still confused. The proof of the Residue Theorem begins with "It is not hard to see" and ends 35 pages later with me questioning every life decision that led to graduate school.

Reimann-Your-Life has achieved something remarkable: a textbook where every chapter is worse than the last, forming a strictly decreasing sequence of quality that itself converges to nothing. The errata PDF is longer than the book. Exercise 4.17 has been proven unsolvable by two Fields Medalists and a particularly gifted border collie. The index contains the entry "hope: see 'delusion'."
